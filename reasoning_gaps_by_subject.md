# AI Reasoning Gaps in K-12/AP Curricula: A DOK-Framed Survey for Fine-Tuning Project Scoping

**Purpose:** Identify, per subject family, question TYPES that (a) require genuine reasoning/logic, (b) are grounded in real K-12/AP curriculum content (not abstract IQ-test puzzles), and (c) show documented or strongly-evidenced AI underperformance — in order to scope a future narrow small-model fine-tuning project analogous to a prior "probability misconception diagnoser" effort.

**How to read this document:** Each subject section has a DOK1 (Facts) layer — discrete, sourced claims — and a DOK2 (Concepts) layer — synthesis of shared patterns and an honest read of evidence strength. UNVERIFIED flags from the underlying research are preserved verbatim; nothing has been invented to fill gaps. A Cross-Subject Synthesis closes the document with recurring reasoning-gap patterns and a ranked shortlist of fine-tuning targets.

## Mathematics (K-12 + AP Calculus AB/BC, AP Statistics, AP Precalculus)

### DOK1 — Facts

- **Multi-step word problems with distractor/irrelevant information** — Grades 4–8 arithmetic/pre-algebra. Example: a kiwi-counting problem where "five of the Sunday kiwis were a bit smaller than average" is an irrelevant clause; models subtracted it anyway. GSM-NoOp evaluation (Mirzadeh et al., "GSM-Symbolic," Apple, ICLR 2025, arXiv:2410.05229): GPT-4o 94.9%→63.1% (−31.8 pts); OpenAI o1-preview 92.7%→77.4% (−15.3 pts); Phi-3.5-mini 82.1%→22.4% (−59.7 pts); Gemma2-9b 79.1%→22.3% (−56.8 pts). Paper frames this as "sophisticated pattern matching" rather than genuine logical reasoning.
- **Geometry problem-solving with diagrams** (angle/length/area requiring diagram-text integration) — Middle school geometry (grades 7–9); GeoQA/Geometry3K/UniGeo sourced from Chinese middle-school exams. GeoQA (arXiv:2105.14517): 4,998 problems, avg. 2.61 solving steps. GeoEval (arXiv:2402.10104): GPT-4 27.95% overall but only **10.10% on "hard" subset**, 26.00% on "backward reasoning" problems; GPT-4V 37.22% overall, collapsing to ~10% on hard problems. MathVista (arXiv:2310.02255): GPT-4V 50.5% on Geometry Problem Solving vs. human baseline 60.3% overall. 2025 follow-up (BiNSGPS, arXiv:2606.04648): GPT-5-class models plateau ~77.9% on Geometry3K's hardest "Completion" mode vs. 90.5% for a specialized neuro-symbolic system.
- **Geometry proofs requiring auxiliary-line construction** — High school geometry proof units (grades 9–10) through competition geometry. GeoLaux (arXiv:2508.06226): 2,186 problems, avg. 6.51 reasoning steps (max 24), 41.8% requiring auxiliary-line construction. **9 of 23 tested MLLMs showed >50% relative performance drop** as required steps increased; models "take shortcuts" on proofs specifically and broadly "lack auxiliary-line awareness."
- **Geometric transformations** (rotation, reflection, folding — infer rule, apply to new shape) — Grade 8 (Common Core 8.G). Visual Reasoning Benchmark (arXiv:2602.12196), built from real primary-school exams (Zambia Grade 7, India Class 6 Navodaya Vidyalaya): folding costs **−9 points**, reflection **−5.8 points**, rotation **−5 points** vs. static tasks; overall accuracy ranged 23% (near chance baseline of 25%) to 78%. Mind's Eye (arXiv:2604.16054): performance drops sharply as composed atomic transformations increase; models struggle to "abstract changes into symbolic rules." "Seeing Isn't Orienting" (arXiv:2505.21649): ~60% joint failure rate on orientation/rotation tasks across architectures, called "fundamental rather than model-specific."
- **Combinatorics/counting with case-splitting** — High school combinatorics/probability, AP Statistics probability unit, AMC/AIME. CombEval (arXiv:2606.19788): "Sequence" (ordered arrangement with constraints) category had **lowest accuracy of any category at 38.0%** vs. 57.9% "Choose," 51.1% "Tuple," 46.5% "Group"; best model (GPT-5.5) only 93.6% overall on controlled problems. CombiBench (arXiv:2505.03171): o1 and DeepSeek-R1 solved close to 0 at Pass@1; best model (Kimina-Prover) solved only 7/100 at Pass@16. MATH-dataset breakdown: Counting & Probability 53.1% vs. Algebra 70.8%/Pre-Algebra 71.6% — **UNVERIFIED, search-derived, not independently fetched from primary source**.
- **Statistics: experimental-design critique** (confounding, causal vs. correlational reasoning) — AP Statistics Unit 3. **No benchmark paper found that directly evaluates LLMs on AP-Statistics-style experimental-design critique with a reported accuracy number** — explicit literature gap. Closest adjacent evidence: CLADDER causal-reasoning benchmark reportedly shows near-random performance on interventional/counterfactual questions (quote UNVERIFIED, sourced from a search snippet, model/accuracy not confirmed). "Context-Aware Reasoning on Parametric Knowledge for Inferring Causal Variables" (arXiv:2409.02604) — genomic domain, only loosely analogous.

### DOK2 — Concepts

- Math's clearest, best-verified pattern is **step-count/complexity sensitivity**: accuracy degrades sharply and non-linearly as reasoning chains lengthen (GeoLaux's 9/23 models with >50% drop; GSM-NoOp's single-clause insertion cutting accuracy by up to 60 points), suggesting models are pattern-completing rather than executing a verifiable procedure.
- A second pattern is **diagram/structure-to-symbol grounding failure**: geometry and transformation tasks show models struggling to correctly bind diagram elements to symbolic reasoning (GeoEval's ~10% hard-subset floor, MaCBench-style isomer confusions in chemistry appear structurally identical — see Cross-Subject Synthesis).
- Evidence strength varies widely within math: the word-problem-distractor and geometry-diagram categories are strongly evidenced with clean, replicated, primary-source-confirmed numbers. Combinatorics case-splitting is well-evidenced for CombEval but has one flagged UNVERIFIED number (MATH dataset 53.1%). The experimental-design-critique category is the weakest in the entire report for math — a **confirmed literature gap**, not just a search failure, and should not be treated as evidenced without new benchmarking.

## English Language Arts / Reading & Writing (K-12 + AP English Language, AP English Literature)

### DOK1 — Facts

- **Referential/syntactic ambiguity resolution** (anaphora, garden-path structures, Winograd-schema style) — Middle school grammar/close-reading (CCSS L.6-8) through AP close reading of complex syntax. Concept-Reversed WSC stress test (2024): GPT-4/Llama-3 accuracy drops **below 80%** when the plausible referent is swapped (arxiv.org/html/2410.12040v1). Correct-Detect benchmark (2025): GPT-4o tuned for ambiguity-detection got 99.55% detection but only **5.23% accuracy on unambiguous cases**; tuned for plain accuracy, 87.7% on unambiguous cases but failed to flag genuine ambiguity; humans held ~77–78% on both simultaneously (arxiv.org/html/2509.14456v2). AmbiEnt (Liu et al. 2023, ACL): GPT-4's disambiguations judged correct only **32% of the time** vs. 90% for gold disambiguations (arxiv.org/abs/2304.14399).
- **Identifying an author's unstated/implicit logical fallacy** — AP English Language (argument/rhetorical analysis FRQs), AP Seminar. RuozhiBench (2025, 17 LLMs): best model (Claude-3-haiku) reached only **62% accuracy** vs. **>90% human accuracy**; other strong models 57–59% (arxiv.org/html/2502.13125v1). A separate 232-fallacy-class study: best model averaged only **~34% accuracy**, with high precision but low recall on fallacies (arxiv.org/html/2410.15050v1).
- **Inferring character motive/reliability contradicting surface text** (unreliable narration, subtext) — High school 9th-10th grade literary analysis; AP Lit prose FRQ. TUNa (ACL 2025): task-difficulty hierarchy where "inter-textual" motive-inference (vs. "intra-narrational" internal-inconsistency spotting) is markedly harder (aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.1013). "Reading Subtext" (TACL): GPT-4, Claude-2.1, Llama-2-70B made faithfulness errors in **over 50% of summaries** of literary short stories, worse on unreliable-narrator/subtext-heavy stories (arxiv.org/abs/2403.01061) — **granular numeric breakdowns UNVERIFIED, PDF extraction failed; qualitative finding from abstract-level language only**.
- **AP Lang-style rhetorical analysis of a passage's line of reasoning** — AP English Language FRQ2, scored on College Board's "Reasoning and Organization" skill. **Strongest single citation in ELA:** OpenAI's GPT-4 Technical Report — GPT-4 scored **2 out of 5 on both AP English Language and Composition (14th–44th percentile) and AP English Literature and Composition (8th–22nd percentile)**, its worst simulated AP exam performance of any subject tested, markedly below AP Biology/Calculus/Chemistry (arxiv.org/abs/2303.08774). ETS study (13,121 student argumentative essays, grades 8-12): GPT-4o average 2.8/6 vs. human average 3.7/6; GPT-4o gave only 3 perfect scores of 6 vs. 732 given by humans (future-ed.org). ArgBench: Llama-3.3-70B, Mistral-22B misclassify argument types when emotional/audience-directed rhetorical appeals are involved (arxiv.org/abs/2604.17366).
- **Poetry structural/formal-logic analysis** (sonnet volta, form enacting meaning) — AP English Literature poetry FRQ. "Sonnet or Not, Bot?" (2024): LLMs good at classifying fixed poetic form but worse at "unfixed" formal/structural elements (arxiv.org/html/2406.18906v1) — **note: tests form classification, not volta comprehension; the specific claim "LLMs fail at volta/turn logical-structure analysis" is UNVERIFIED as a benchmarked claim**, a reasonable extrapolation only.
- **Multi-hop reasoning under ambiguity / contradictory textual clues held in tension** — AP Lit synthesis of conflicting characterizations; AP Seminar/Research. MARCH (arXiv:2509.22750, 2,209 questions): frontier models "struggle" when ambiguity interpretation and multi-hop inference combine — **exact accuracy percentages UNVERIFIED, PDF extraction failed, qualitative-only finding**. Related work on knowledge-conflict/RAG contexts shows recency/primacy bias overriding contradiction reconciliation (arxiv.org/pdf/2604.11209) — **extrapolation to literary close-reading, not directly tested**.

### DOK2 — Concepts

- The single strongest, most citable data point in ELA — and arguably in the entire cross-subject survey — is the **GPT-4 Technical Report's AP English scores (2/5 on both exams)**, because it comes from a primary, well-known source, uses real exam scoring, and shows a stark contrast with GPT-4's near-ceiling performance on STEM AP exams in the *same report*. This is a clean natural experiment: recall-heavy exams are solved, reasoning-about-argument-structure exams are not.
- A recurring sub-pattern is **"detect X while also not over-flagging X"** — the Correct-Detect ambiguity-detection/accuracy tradeoff shows models cannot hold two calibrated judgments simultaneously (either over-flag ambiguity or under-flag it), which is a distinct and interesting failure shape from simple accuracy degradation.
- Evidence strength is uneven: rhetorical-analysis-and-grading (AP Lang) and fallacy-identification are strongly evidenced with primary-source-confirmed numbers. Ambiguity resolution is well-evidenced. Unreliable-narrator/subtext inference and multi-hop-under-ambiguity are qualitatively well-supported but numerically thin (multiple UNVERIFIED flags due to PDF extraction failures, not absence of studies). The sonnet-volta category is the weakest — no direct benchmark exists; treat it as an open research question, not a documented gap.

## Biology & Life Sciences (K-12 + AP Biology, AP Environmental Science)

### DOK1 — Facts

- **Ecological perturbation / food-web cascade reasoning** — Middle school ecosystems unit through AP Environmental Science/AP Biology (trophic cascades). bioRxiv ecological-knowledge study (Feb 2025, GPT-4o & Gemini 1.5 Pro): models beat naive baselines by only ~20 points on species-presence prediction, reached **about a third of the mean F1 score** naive baselines achieve on range-map generation, improved threat classification by only **~10 points over random guessing** (bioRxiv 10.1101/2025.02.10.637097). Related food-web construction study: **51–59% consistency** identifying trophic interaction links vs. an expert-derived food web (vs. >99.7% reproducibility on simpler species-grouping) — **UNVERIFIED, could not independently confirm via direct fetch, single-source**.
- **Biological pathway/signaling-diagram perturbation reasoning** ("what happens if step X is blocked") — AP Biology cell signaling/gene regulation units. **Strongest-evidenced biology category.** BioMaze (arXiv:2502.16660, 5,100 questions from real research literature): best LLM accuracy **42.9% vs. 74.0% human baseline** (31-point gap); True/False sub-tasks only slightly above random chance; paper explicitly states LLMs "struggle with pathway reasoning, especially in perturbed systems." Separate multimodal pathway-diagram study: GPT-4oV led with only **0.687 F1-score** on directional interaction prediction from images, Llama-3.2V 0.656, Claude-3.5V 0.641, Gemini-1.5V 0.573 — **PLAUSIBLE, drawn from WebSearch summary, PMC primary page not directly opened**.
- **Experimental design critique** (confounds, missing controls, validity threats) — Middle school basic control-group ID through AP Biology/AP Environmental Science FRQs. BioLP-Bench (bioRxiv, embedded fatal protocol errors): **human expert baseline 38.4% accuracy**; o4-mini 45.1%, Claude-4-sonnet 44.6% (bioRxiv 10.1101/2024.08.21.608694). "Ice Cream Doesn't Cause Drowning" (arXiv:2505.13770): best model (GPT-o4-mini) only **43.03% average causal reliability**; conclusions flippable by irrelevant surface features (e.g., brand name); hardest tier direct-prompting accuracy as low as **17.84%** (30.26% with code-assisted prompting). **Caveat: BioLP-Bench is professional/grad-level, treated as a proxy for the same skill, not a direct K-12/AP benchmark — flagged UNVERIFIED at the K-12/AP level specifically.**
- **Multi-generation genetics cross reasoning** (dihybrid crosses, epistasis, pedigree inference) — Grade 9-10 (monohybrid) through AP Biology (dihybrid, epistasis, pedigree). **Weakest-evidenced category in biology and possibly the whole report: no benchmark found with reported accuracy numbers despite multiple targeted searches.** GPQA biology component reported inconsistently (~20 genetics-specific questions within an ~85–105 question biology pool out of 448 total — exact figures vary by source, both "78" and "105" treated as approximate); GPT-4 overall GPQA score 39% vs. 65% PhD experts (arXiv:2311.12022) — not broken out by genetics sub-accuracy, so cannot support a genetics-specific claim. **Explicit flag: any specific LLM accuracy percentage on dihybrid-cross/epistasis problems should be treated as fabricated unless a source is produced.**
- **Trait-table/dichotomous-key classification of an unknown organism** — Grade 6-8 classification unit through Biology 1 taxonomy/cladistics. Biodiversity-classification study (21,955 IUCN Red List species): 2024-2025 models clustered with mean balanced accuracies **above 0.76** — PLAUSIBLE, direct fetch blocked (403), single-source via search summary. "Taxonomic Reasoning for Rare Arthropods" (arXiv:2503.10886): retrieval-augmentation gave only "small boost" for rare species at family/genus level; failure modes range "from superficial visual matching to biologically implausible trait references." **No benchmark uses classroom-style text-based dichotomous keys — indirect/analog evidence only.**

### DOK2 — Concepts

- The clearest single throughline in biology: **the gap between LLMs and experts widens sharply as questions move from "grade-school ScienceQA-style" (near-ceiling, 86.5–92.5% for top models) to "graduate-level/perturbation/multi-hop" (GPQA, BioMaze, BioLP-Bench — all in the 40–55% range for best models)**. This gap-widening-with-reasoning-depth pattern, not raw content difficulty, appears to be the operative variable.
- Pathway/signaling perturbation reasoning (BioMaze) is the best-evidenced, most severe, and most clearly curriculum-relevant biology gap: a 31-point human-model gap on a task structurally identical to "if you knock out gene/step X, what happens downstream" — a staple of AP Bio.
- Genetics-cross/pedigree reasoning is intuitively one of the most promising candidates for a fine-tuning project (compound classification + probability calculation, exactly analogous to the prior probability-misconception project) but **has zero direct benchmark evidence** — this is a genuine research gap that would need to be filled by original evaluation before committing to it as a project target.
- Overall evidence strength in biology is moderate: BioMaze and BioLP-Bench are strong (multi-source corroborated, primary-fetched); ecological-cascade and classification evidence is single-source/PLAUSIBLE; genetics-cross evidence is absent.

## Chemistry & Physics (K-12 + AP Chemistry, AP Physics 1/2/C)

### DOK1 — Facts

- **Circuit diagram fault/missing-component reasoning** — AP Physics 1/2, AP Physics C: E&M; grade 9-10 physical science. MMCircuitEval (3,614 QA pairs): weak VLMs (InstructBLIP, BLIP2) **below 20% accuracy**; GPT-4o 75.9% on basic comprehension but GPT-4V only **48.2% on backend-design reasoning** despite 69.4% overall; 2.2–13.5% decline moving text-only→multimodal; "circuit-recognition hallucinations" including misread source polarity (arxiv.org/html/2507.19525v1). AP Physics FRQ study (arxiv.org/html/2603.07457v1) independently found "circuit topology" confusion as a recurring error category across Claude/GPT/Gemini/DeepSeek on real AP Physics FRQs (2015-2025) despite 82-92% aggregate scores. **UNVERIFIED: no benchmark isolates "missing component" fault diagnosis specifically with a clean number.**
- **Multi-step stoichiometry with limiting-reagent case analysis** — 10th-11th grade Chemistry/AP Chemistry Unit 4. SciBench (869 college-level free-response problems): best overall LLM score only **43.22%** (GPT-4 + Python tool, few-shot) (arxiv.org/abs/2307.10635). **UNVERIFIED: no benchmark isolates limiting-reagent case-analysis as its own category; 43.22% is the broader chemistry+physics+math figure, not limiting-reagent-specific.**
- **Free-body-diagram force reasoning under changing constraints** — AP Physics 1 (Newton's Laws), AP Physics C: Mechanics. ABench-Physics (Phy_A static vs. Phy_B dynamically-varied, 100 problems): **22.5% average accuracy drop** moving from static to parameter-varied versions; static high-difficulty top scores: Gemini 2.5 Pro (reasoning) 43.0%, o3 37.8%, Claude 3.7 Sonnet (thinking) 22.0%, GPT-4o 10.0% (arxiv.org/html/2507.04766v1) — **note: some post-perturbation numbers in the fetched summary look like an extraction artifact inconsistent with the paper's own "drop" framing; the 22.5%-average-drop headline is treated as verified since it's the paper's stated conclusion, but raw post-perturbation percentages are UNVERIFIED**. Related mechanistic study using pulley systems (arxiv.org/pdf/2507.15521) found LLMs pattern-match rather than derive forces from first principles under novel component arrangements — **specific numeric error rates UNVERIFIED, qualitative claim only**.
- **Reasoning about a described chemical mechanism's rate-limiting step** — AP Chemistry Kinetics unit. oMeBench (10,000+ expert-annotated organic mechanism steps, oMeS partial-credit metric): Gemini-2.5-Pro 37.9, GPT-5 29.1, o3 28.1, Claude-Sonnet-4 17.9, GPT-4o 5.0 (out of 100) (arxiv.org/abs/2510.07731) — paper's conclusion: models "struggle with correct and consistent multi-step reasoning" as mechanisms lengthen; fine-tuning improved a specialist model by 50% relative to best closed-source baseline. **Note: arXiv listed as possibly "withdrawn" in one metadata check — status itself UNVERIFIED, but HTML v1 full text retrievable and internally consistent. Rate-determining-step-specific isolation is UNVERIFIED as a standalone statistic** (oMeBench measures full mechanistic pathway accuracy).
- **Titration curve inflection-point / general quantitative graph reading** — AP Chemistry Acid-Base Equilibria unit. **No benchmark found testing titration-curve interpretation directly — UNVERIFIED as a directly-measured category.** Analogous evidence: AP Physics FRQ study found models "failed to read correct pressure and volume coordinates from the graph" as a major recurring error type. MaCBench: XRD "identify highest peak" 0.74 accuracy but "rank relative peak intensities" dropped to **0.28**; NMR/mass-spec analysis averaged only **35%**; AFM image interpretation only **24%**.
- **3D molecular geometry/chirality/stereochemistry from a 2D drawing** — AP Chemistry Bonding & Molecular Structure (VSEPR); stereochemistry at the AP Chem boundary, heavy in intro organic chem. MaCBench: "isomer relationship identification" (same compound/enantiomer/diastereomer) scored only **24% accuracy** averaged across 4 frontier VLLMs, among the worst tasks in the entire benchmark, vs. 80% on "hand-drawn molecule to SMILES" matching. AtomWorld (arxiv.org/html/2510.04704v2): rotation-based spatial transformation success rates **below 12%** in the "hard" tier — **finer per-model splits UNVERIFIED, page extraction incomplete**. USNCO Chemistry Olympiad benchmark (arxiv.org/abs/2512.14989, 40 models): GPT-5 93.2% macro-average, 86.3% on 2025 exam vs. ~44.6% estimated human benchmark — but **"removing the image even improves accuracy" for some models** (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-3B improved +2.4 points with the image removed), indicating high aggregate scores co-exist with models frequently not actually using the structural diagram.

### DOK2 — Concepts

- Chemistry/physics shows a distinctive and well-evidenced pattern: **static-to-dynamic (parameter-varied) collapse**. ABench-Physics's 22.5% average drop when the same problem structure is re-parameterized is direct evidence that models are retrieving memorized problem-solution mappings rather than re-deriving from first principles — a mechanistically important finding for fine-tuning scoping (it implies training-set contamination/memorization is doing real work in baseline scores).
- A second, chemistry-specific pattern is **diagram-ignoring / vision-language misalignment**: the USNCO finding that removing the image *improves* accuracy for some models is a uniquely clean and damning piece of evidence — it demonstrates models can look competent on stereochemistry questions while structurally not using the picture at all, which is a distinct failure mode from "gets the answer wrong."
- The 24% isomer-relationship and <12% rotation-operation numbers (MaCBench, AtomWorld) make 3D chirality/stereochemistry the single most severely-failing, most cleanly-quantified chemistry category.
- Evidence strength: MaCBench, oMeBench, SciBench, and ABench-Physics are all strongly evidenced (primary-fetched, internally consistent). Circuit fault-diagnosis, limiting-reagent-specific, and titration-curve categories are weaker — real adjacent evidence exists but the exact question type is not isolated in any benchmark.

## Earth & Environmental Science (K-12 + AP Environmental Science)

### DOK1 — Facts

- **Topographic map cross-referencing → geological/landform history inference** — Middle school Earth science (NGSS MS-ESS2) through AP Environmental Science. **Strongest-evidenced Earth-science category.** MapEval (ICML 2025, arXiv:2501.00316, 30 models, 700 map-based MCQs): **no model exceeded 67% accuracy**; Claude 3.5 Sonnet led at ~66.3%, all models trailed human accuracy (~86.7%) by over 20 points. PEACE/GeoMap-Bench (CVPR 2025, arXiv:2501.06184, 124 real geologic maps, 3,864 MCQs): GPT-4o scored only **0.369 overall** vs. 0.811 for a specialized agent; per-category — Extracting 0.219, Grounding 0.128, Referring 0.378, Reasoning 0.507, Analyzing 0.612 (basic visual-parsing tasks scored *worse* than higher-level reasoning). **Caveat: does not test event-sequencing/cross-cutting relationships — explicitly flagged by the benchmark's own authors as future work.** SpatialEval (NeurIPS 2024): VLMs often underperform text-only counterparts on map/spatial tasks; accuracy degrades sharply as spatial complexity increases.
- **Weather map/pressure-system diagram → predicting future conditions** — Middle/high school weather unit; AP Environmental Science/Physical Geography. "Pixels and Predictions" (AMS *AIES* 2025, arXiv:2404.15166): GPT-4V **hallucinated details not present in weather charts**, showed inconsistent self-evaluation — qualitative/expert-graded, no single clean accuracy percentage. SynopticBench (arXiv:2604.16451, **2026 preprint, not peer-reviewed**): low overlap scores vs. real NWS forecast discussions across all tested VLMs — **UNVERIFIED/early**. AgentCaster/TornadoBench (arXiv:2510.03349): LLM agents scored **below 10% on TornadoBench**, "strong tendency toward hallucinations" including misplaced risk areas at rates higher than human SPC baseline — **note: severe convective forecasting is a much harder/more specialized task than K-12 "which way does the front move," treat 10% as an upper-bound-difficulty analog, not grade-level equivalent.**
- **Multi-factor systems reasoning: net effect of two opposing feedback loops** — AP Environmental Science climate systems unit. **No benchmark tests this directly — explicit gap.** Borrowed evidence: ChaosBench-Logic v2 (arXiv:2605.24305, **May 2026 preprint, not peer-reviewed**, abstract math dynamical systems not climate-specific): "regime-transition reasoning" scored near-random (MCC ≈ 0.05) even for frontier models vs. simple deduction (MCC ≈ 0.52); two models showed *negative* MCC on bifurcation questions. CounterLogic/CounterBench (arXiv:2505.11839, arXiv:2502.11008): accuracy drops from ~72% on knowledge-aligned reasoning to ~58% on counterfactual reasoning (14-point drop, 11 models) — **all borrowed/analogical evidence, not environmental-science-specific**. Claim that "LLMs default to linear single-cause explanations" for systems questions is **UNVERIFIED** — no quantifying benchmark found.
- **Stratigraphy/rock-layer cross-section → sequencing geological events (relative dating)** — Middle school Earth science (NGSS MS-ESS1/ESS2), intro high school geology. **Weakest-evidenced category in Earth science and possibly the whole report: no benchmark found testing this exact skill.** GeoMap-Bench/PEACE explicitly excludes relative-dating/cross-cutting-relationship sequencing, listing it as future work. Borrowed evidence only: PEACE's low Grounding (0.128) and Extracting (0.219) scores suggest visual-parsing prerequisites are already weak; ChaosBench-Logic v2 and CounterLogic's general step-count degradation findings are offered as structural analogs, **not stratigraphy-specific**.
- **Population/carrying-capacity graph → non-obvious "what happens next" prediction** — Middle school ecosystems unit, AP Environmental Science Population Ecology. **No dedicated benchmark found — explicit gap.** Borrowed from chart-QA literature: original ChartQA now near-ceiling for top models (Claude 3.5 Sonnet 90.5%, Qwen2.5-VL 89.5%, GPT-4/Gemini 85-87%) but only for simple value-lookup, not inferential "what does this curve shape imply" questions. Follow-on benchmarks ChartQAPro (arXiv:2504.05506) and ChartMind (arXiv:2505.23242) report substantially lower accuracy on more open-ended questions, but **no clean top-line percentage could be retrieved — numerically UNVERIFIED**.

### DOK2 — Concepts

- Earth science has the most explicit and largest number of confirmed literature gaps of any subject in this report: 3 of 5 question types (feedback-loop reasoning, stratigraphy sequencing, carrying-capacity prediction) have **no direct benchmark** and rely entirely on borrowed/analogical evidence from abstract dynamical-systems logic, counterfactual-reasoning benchmarks, or chart-QA studies.
- Where direct evidence exists (topographic/geologic map reasoning), it is strong and multi-source: MapEval and PEACE/GeoMap-Bench both show a >20-point human-model gap and a ceiling around 66-67% even for the best models, with basic visual grounding (not just high-level reasoning) failing.
- The stratigraphy/relative-dating category is flagged by the researcher as intuitively one of the strongest candidates (dense multi-rule visual sequencing: superposition + horizontality + cross-cutting + unconformity, applied simultaneously) despite having zero direct evidence — this is explicitly a "promising but unvalidated" case requiring original benchmarking before it could support a fine-tuning project claim.
- Overall Earth science evidence base is the thinnest of the STEM subjects in this report; two of the cited sources are 2026 preprints not yet peer-reviewed (SynopticBench, ChaosBench-Logic v2), which should be weighted accordingly.

## History & Social Studies (K-12 + AP US History, AP World History, AP European History, AP Human Geography)

### DOK1 — Facts

- **DBQ reasoning with contradictory primary sources + bias attribution** — AP US/World/European History DBQ format; scaffolded in NY Regents/CA middle-high school document-based units. Twaddell informal study (Concordia Shanghai, ~6 essays/type, ChatGPT vs. real AP rubrics): **DBQ average score 1 out of 7; LEQ averaged 2 out of 6** — attributed to inability to use "the specific primary sources provided" and demonstrate sourcing/corroboration skills. **Explicitly small/informal (n≈6), not peer-reviewed — numbers illustrative, not statistically robust**, but consistent with academic literature below. Benchmarking as Source Criticism (JOHD 2024/2025): current benchmarks "fail to expose... inability to resolve conflicting sources"; experts "dominate in any context requiring multi-source synthesis." ProHist-Bench (400 expert-curated Chinese-history questions, 10,891 rubric criteria): top models (Gemini-3-Pro, Qwen3-235B) rubric hit-rates "rarely exceeding 0.23" on viewpoint-integration/evidentiary-analysis tasks; performance "collapses" on contradictory-evidence synthesis.
- **Sourcing/corroboration reasoning** (which source is more reliable, from internal clues alone) — Middle/high school US History/Civics (SHEG "Reading Like a Historian," Civic Online Reasoning); foundational AP DBQ skill. "Source framing triggers systematic bias in large language models" (Science Advances): LLM credibility judgments shift systematically based on superficial source-attribution framing rather than substance. HiBenchLLM (14 LLMs, regional French history, 62 open-ended questions): interpretive-synthesis questions scored **16% reliability** vs. **90%+ on contamination-vulnerable MC recall — a ~5x drop-off**. **Any claim that SHEG has directly published a study testing LLM chatbots is UNVERIFIED — only human-subject digital-literacy studies located.**
- **Causal-chain reasoning** (proximate vs. root cause) — AP US/World History "Historical Causation" official skill; middle-school WWI causes units. ### DOK2 — Concepts

- History's cleanest quantified pattern is the **~5x recognition-vs-reasoning gap** (HiBenchLLM: 90%+ on MC recall vs. 16% on interpretive synthesis), which directly parallels the GPT-4 Technical Report's own internal contrast (5/5 full AP US History exam vs. 1/7 DBQ-only informal score) — two independent sources converging on the same shape of gap.

### DOK2 — Concepts

- World Languages has an unusually explicit, stated meta-finding: **no benchmark reviewed was built directly from AP exam items or a K-12 curriculum bank** — every number is borrowed from adjacent computational-linguistics research on the same cognitive operation (anaphora resolution, syntactic attachment, idiom disambiguation, formality control), which the researcher explicitly labels as an inferential leap, not a direct claim by the cited papers.

### DOK1 — Facts

CRUXEval (Gu et al., NeurIPS 2024, 800 Python functions): GPT-4 (best tested) only **63% pass@1 on output prediction**, 67% on input prediction without CoT (81.9% with CoT on output prediction); CodeLlama-34B only 44-47% (crux-eval.github.io). CodeMind (arXiv:2402.09664, 10 LLMs, 5,395 programs): performance drops specifically for **non-primitive types (objects, arrays)** — exactly where aliasing bugs live, though no single universal percentage given for this subcategory.

"Assessing the Impact of Code Changes on Fault Localizability of LLMs" (arXiv:2504.04372, ICST 2026): Claude Sonnet 4.5 correctly localized an N-Queens off-by-one bug initially, but after **semantic-preserving mutations** (renaming variables/functions, adding misleading comments — no behavior change) **failed to find the same bug**, flagging an unrelated line instead. - **Big-O/algorithmic-complexity reasoning about a novel algorithm** — AP CSA basic complexity intuition; AP CSP "algorithmic efficiency" Big Idea; rigorous treatment in dual-enrollment Data Structures. BigO(Bench) (Meta/FAIR, arXiv:2503.15242, 3,105 problems, 1,190,250 solutions, complexity inferred via profiling not textbook-labeled): "token-space reasoning models are unrivaled in code generation but not in complexity understanding" — models that generate working code frequently cannot correctly predict/satisfy that same code's Big-O behavior. LiveCodeBench (arXiv:2403.07974): models show a "stark accuracy drop on problems released after their training cutoff date," direct evidence that apparent algorithmic reasoning is partly memorization.
- **Finite-state-machine/automaton trace-through problems** — AP CSP/CS Unplugged (down to elementary via "Pirates' Treasure Hunt" unplugged FSM activity); implicit in AP CSA state-holding loop tracing. "The Illusion of Procedural Reasoning" (arXiv:2511.14777, Nov 2025): models "mimic algorithmic behavior for short traces but fail to sustain coherent execution as procedural depth grows," degradation worse for high branching complexity than for longer chains alone. "Beyond Memorization: Testing LLM Reasoning on Unseen Theory of Computation Tasks" (arXiv:2601.13392, Jan 2026): models scored **84-90% on previously-seen problems** but accuracy **dropped by 30-64 percentage points on novel (unseen) automaton tasks**, with recurring Kleene-star semantic errors and failure to preserve global state consistency; errors persisted across direct, CoT, and tree-of-thought prompting.
- **Recursive-function trace reasoning with non-obvious base cases** — AP CSA recursion unit (Strings/arrays/ArrayLists per College Board framework); Collatz-like traces. arXiv:2512.00215's error taxonomy explicitly names **"failure to correctly expand recursive calls"** as one of nine categorized statement-level reasoning failure types. **UNVERIFIED/not separately confirmed: no benchmark isolates recursion-specific accuracy as a standalone published statistic (e.g., recursive vs. iterative trace accuracy) — treat any specific recursion-only percentage as unverified.**

### DOK2 — Concepts

- CS has the cleanest, most rigorously multi-source-verified "novel vs. seen" collapse pattern of any subject: **84-90% (seen) → drops of 30-64 points (unseen automaton tasks)** in arXiv:2601.13392, and LiveCodeBench's contamination-sensitive accuracy drop on post-cutoff problems — both are direct, quantified evidence that baseline competence is substantially inflated by memorization rather than genuine procedural-execution ability.

- Evidence strength in CS is uniformly strong across categories (CRUXEval, CodeMind, BigO(Bench), the fault-localizability paper, and the FSM/automaton papers are all primary-fetched and mutually corroborating); the only explicitly weak sub-claim is isolating recursion-specific accuracy as its own number, and one informal/unverified claim about a specific AP CSA FRQ score (32/36, sourced to an ungraded GitHub gist, explicitly flagged low-rigor).

MedFuzz: inserting realistic-but-distracting patient characteristics ("unlikely to fool medical experts") reliably flips LLMs from correct to incorrect. **Inferential leap explicitly flagged as large: these are dense adult-clinical contexts; whether the same failure modes persist at simpler K-12 risk-factor counts is UNVERIFIED.**
- **Biomechanical reasoning about unsafe/inefficient movement technique** (lever/force reasoning applied to human movement) — Middle/high school; maps to SHAPE America Standard 2. "Probing Mechanical Reasoning in Large Vision Language Models" (arxiv.org/pdf/2410.00318, GPT-4o/Gemini/Claude/LLaVA/Qwen-VL, six mechanical domains): **humans ~90% on pulley-system reasoning vs. ~50% average for models** — but the paper's "human movement" content is limited to object/inertia scenarios, **not literal human-body biomechanics; confirmed no dedicated human-movement task category exists**. PhySense (arxiv.org/pdf/2505.24823): LLMs "assert nontrivial formulas without derivation," show "incomplete grasp or misapplication of physical principles." **Inferential leap: moderate-to-large**, since the K-12 example (single-principle lever reasoning) is much lower-dimensional than tested multi-object pulley/gear systems.
- **Nutrition-label multi-constraint reasoning** (jointly satisfying calorie/fiber/sodium constraints with serving-size conversion) — Upper elementary through high school; maps to NHES Standards 1 and 5. **No study found testing LLMs on nutrition-label constraint-satisfaction directly — explicitly the weakest-evidenced category in this subject.** LR²Bench (arxiv.org/pdf/2502.17848, abstract logic puzzles/Sudoku-variants): DeepSeek-R1 averaged only **20.0% exact-match**, o1-preview **23.6%**, with a "giving-up moment" failure mode. Nutritionist-licensing-exam study: GPT-4o's incorrect answers "more frequently associated with numerical terms" (pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12702670). Diet-optimization-problem study (arxiv.org/pdf/2510.16943): variable constraint precision/recall by model — **specific figures UNVERIFIED, PDF garbled, secondhand via search summary only**. **Inferential leap: large — abstract CSP puzzles and adult diet-optimization math are structurally different from a grade-6, 2-3-constraint label task.**
- **Physiological cause-effect chains** (training/injury scenarios, e.g., overload principle → stress reaction → prognosis) — High school primarily; maps to SHAPE Standard 2 (FITT) and NHES Standard 1. "Failure Modes of LLMs for Causal Reasoning on Narratives" (arxiv.org/pdf/2410.23884, GPT-4/Claude 3.5/Llama): identifies **positional bias** (assuming causes precede effects in presentation order; fails when narrative order is reversed) and **parametric knowledge override** (when a narrative's specific causal claim conflicts with general world knowledge, model ignores the narrative and defaults to memorized generic knowledge) — **exact quantitative figures UNVERIFIED, could not extract from PDF text layer; qualitative taxonomy only**. Multi-hop QA research (arxiv.org/pdf/2601.12499): reasoning chains collapse to the level of "the least visible evidence" (weakest-link effect). **Inferential leap: moderate** — failure modes demonstrated on general narrative/QA tasks, not physiology specifically.

### DOK2 — Concepts

- Health/PE is, by the researcher's own explicit framing, the **thinnest evidence base of any subject family in this entire report** — zero direct K-12/PE-curriculum LLM studies exist, and every claim is a labeled analogical extrapolation from a different domain (clinical medicine, abstract mechanical-reasoning benchmarks, abstract constraint-satisfaction puzzles, general narrative-causal-reasoning studies).
- The strongest quantified adjacent number (90% human vs. ~50% model on pulley/lever mechanical reasoning) is explicitly noted to not even be a human-movement task — it's object physics, making the leverage-to-biomechanics claim doubly indirect (both cross-domain and cross-context).
- The nutrition-label and physiological-cause-effect-chain categories both have plausible mechanism-level support (constraint-satisfaction collapse in LR²Bench; positional-bias/parametric-override failure modes in narrative causal reasoning) but no health-specific instantiation has been tested — these should be treated as hypotheses for original evaluation, not documented findings.
- Given the near-total absence of direct evidence, Health/PE is not a strong candidate for an evidence-based fine-tuning project without first commissioning original benchmarking — the researcher's own framing supports treating this subject as out of scope for now.

## Cross-Subject Synthesis

### Recurring reasoning-gap patterns (DOK2, cross-subject)

The following patterns recur across three or more subject families, suggesting they reflect general architectural/training limitations rather than subject-specific knowledge gaps:

| # | Pattern | Subjects where documented | Representative evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Diagram/spatial state-tracing** — binding symbolic reasoning correctly to a diagram's structure (geometry figures, circuit schematics, pathway diagrams, molecular structures, topographic/geologic maps, historical maps) | Math, Chemistry/Physics, Biology, Earth Science, History, CS (FSM diagrams) | GeoEval 10.1% on hard geometry; MMCircuitEval 48.2% backend-design reasoning; BioMaze 42.9% pathway reasoning; PEACE/GeoMap-Bench 0.369 overall; USNCO "removing the image improves accuracy" |
| 2 | **Novel/unseen-instance collapse (vs. seen/memorized instance)** — accuracy drops sharply when the same skill is tested on a structurally-varied or post-training-cutoff instance instead of a familiar/memorized one | Math, Chemistry/Physics, CS, Music | ABench-Physics 22.5-point static→dynamic drop; CS automaton tasks 84-90%→drops of 30-64 pts on unseen tasks; LiveCodeBench post-cutoff drop; GSM-NoOp irrelevant-clause drop up to 60 pts |
| 3 | **Case-split / combinatorial branching reasoning** — correctly enumerating or selecting the right sub-case (limiting reagent, ordered-arrangement counting, game-tree composition, constraint-satisfaction with multiple simultaneous constraints) | Math, Chemistry, Government/Econ, Health (adjacent) | CombEval Sequence-type 38.0%; TMGBench compositional games drop to 35-70%; LR²Bench 20-23.6% exact-match |
| 4 | **Contradictory/conflicting-evidence weighing** — reconciling two sources, characterizations, or causal claims that point in different directions, rather than defaulting to one, averaging, or picking the more detailed one | ELA, History, Government/Econ (context-shift) | AmbiEnt 32% correct disambiguation vs. 90% gold; HiBenchLLM 16% interpretive synthesis vs. 90%+ MC recall; ProHist-Bench ≤0.23 hit rate; EconCausal 73.9%→41.3% under context shift |
| 5 | **Counterfactual / interventional reasoning** ("what if X had not happened," "what happens if step Y is blocked/removed") | Math (adjacent), Biology (pathway perturbation), Earth Science (feedback loops, borrowed), History, Government/Econ | CounterBench ~50% (chance-level); BioMaze 42.9% vs 74% human on perturbed systems; CLadder 64.28-70.40% vs 49.27% random baseline |
| 6 | **Surface-feature/pattern-matching substituting for genuine logic** — models are demonstrably swayed by irrelevant surface changes (variable renaming, misleading comments, source-framing, brand names) that don't change the underlying logical structure | Math, CS, Government/Econ, History | GSM-NoOp irrelevant clause (up to −59.7 pts); CS fault-localization 78% failure rate after semantic-preserving mutation; EconCausal swayed by brand-name framing; "Source framing triggers systematic bias" (Science Advances) |
| 7 | **Knowledge/recall vs. reasoning/application split within the same domain** — models score much higher on naming/classifying/recalling a concept than on applying it to a novel instance | Math, ELA, Music, CS, Chemistry | MusicTheoryBench 58.20% Knowledge vs 25.60% Reasoning; GPT-4 Technical Report 5/5 full AP US History exam vs. 1/7 informal DBQ-only score; oMeBench "chemical intuition" present but multi-step mechanism reasoning fails |
| 8 | **Anglocentric/majority-pattern default override** — when a target structure (language, institution, or convention) diverges from the most common training-data pattern, models default to the common pattern rather than the locally-correct one | World Languages, Government/Econ (institutional prediction), Chemistry (chirality: ignoring the image) | Korean relative-clause attachment >60% wrong (English-default); "Thinking Fast, Thinking Wrong" 38-39% on counter-intuitive policy cases; USNCO image-removal improving scores |

### Ranked shortlist: most promising (subject, question-type) pairs for a future fine-tuning project

Evaluated against the criteria that made the prior probability-misconception project strong: **(a)** fails reliably and is well-evidenced ("fails the prompt test"), **(b)** has clean, auto-verifiable ground truth, **(c)** is a narrow, well-defined behavior (not a sprawling skill).

1. **CS — Code tracing with mutable-state/aliasing traps (reference vs. copy semantics)**
   *Justification:* Strongest fit on all three criteria. Evidence is severe and multi-source (CRUXEval 63-67% pass@1; arXiv:2512.00215 naming aliasing/reference-vs-copy as a specific named failure mode most models fail). Ground truth is perfectly auto-verifiable — just execute the code and compare printed output, no human judgment needed. Scope is narrow and precisely definable (a specific Java/Python semantic trap taught explicitly in AP CSA). This most closely mirrors the "probability misconception diagnoser" shape: a specific, nameable misconception (treating a reference as a copy) with a mechanical right answer.

2. **CS — Finding a subtle logical bug that survives semantic-preserving surface mutation**
   *Justification:* Second-strongest: the 78%-failure-rate-after-mutation finding (arXiv:2504.04372) is a clean, quantified, causally-isolated experiment (same bug, only cosmetic changes, correct answer flips to wrong) — arguably an even cleaner "prompt test" than #1 because it directly demonstrates non-robust reasoning. Ground truth is auto-verifiable (a documented planted bug with a known location/fix). Slightly broader scope than #1 (spans multiple bug types) but still tractable.

3. **Math — Geometry proofs/problems requiring auxiliary-line construction or diagram-symbol grounding**
   *Justification:* GeoEval's 10.1% hard-subset floor and GeoLaux's "9/23 models >50% drop as steps increase" are severe, multi-source-corroborated failures on real (Chinese middle-school exam-sourced) curriculum content. Ground truth is auto-verifiable via geometric theorem-checkers/symbolic solvers. Scope is moderately narrow (a specific class of "must add a construction not given in the figure" problems) though somewhat broader than the CS candidates since it spans several sub-skills (angle-chasing, congruence, circle theorems).

4. **Chemistry — 3D chirality/stereochemistry reasoning from 2D structural drawings (isomer relationship identification)**
   *Justification:* MaCBench's 24% isomer-relationship accuracy (worst-performing task in that benchmark) plus AtomWorld's <12% rotation-operation success and the USNCO finding that removing the image *improves* some models' scores together make an unusually strong, convergent case. Ground truth is cleanly auto-verifiable (same/enantiomer/diastereomer relationships are a deterministic function of molecular graph + stereocenters — checkable by RDKit-style cheminformatics tools). Scope is narrow (a single, well-defined classification task: same compound vs. enantiomer vs. diastereomer).

5. **Math — Combinatorics case-splitting on ordered-arrangement ("Sequence"-type) counting problems**
   *Justification:* CombEval's clean 38.0% accuracy on Sequence-type problems (lowest of all categories, vs. 57.9% "Choose") is a well-defined, single-benchmark, single-paper number from a controlled, solver-verified problem set. Ground truth is trivially auto-verifiable (combinatorics problems have exact numeric answers). This directly parallels the "probability misconception" project in domain (counting/probability) and in mechanism (systematic over/undercounting due to a specific reasoning error — treating indistinguishable elements as distinguishable, or vice versa), making it an unusually close spiritual sequel to the prior project. Scope is narrow if restricted specifically to adjacency/ordering-constraint counting problems.

**Honorable mention, not ranked in the top 5 due to evidence gaps rather than lack of promise:** Biology multi-generation genetics-cross/pedigree reasoning (dihybrid crosses, epistasis) and Earth Science stratigraphic relative-dating sequencing are both structurally excellent candidates (compound rule-application + auto-verifiable ground truth + narrow scope) but currently have **zero direct benchmark evidence** — before either could be scoped as a fine-tuning project target, original evaluation would need to be run to confirm the hypothesized gap actually exists at the expected severity.

### Overall evidence-quality caveat

Evidence strength varies substantially by subject: CS, Math (geometry/word-problems), and Chemistry/Physics rest on multiple primary-fetched, mutually-corroborating, large-scale benchmarks. ELA, Biology (pathway reasoning), and World Languages (idiom/attachment) have strong headline numbers but thinner replication. History, Government/Econ, Arts, and especially Earth Science and Health/PE lean heavily on borrowed/analogical evidence from adjacent domains, with explicit, repeated UNVERIFIED flags for the K-12/AP-specific instantiation of the claimed skill. No subject in this report has a benchmark built directly from real AP exam item banks at scale (the closest exceptions are the informal, small-n Twaddell DBQ study and the GPT-4 Technical Report's full-exam AP scores) — this is a structural limitation of the current literature, not a gap unique to any one research pass, and should inform how much independent verification is budgeted before finalizing a fine-tuning project scope.
